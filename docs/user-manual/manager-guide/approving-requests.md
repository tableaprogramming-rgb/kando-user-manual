# Approving Requests

*Manage and approve employee requests including leave, swaps, adjustments, and other formal requests.*

## Overview

The Approvals feature allows you to:
- View all pending employee requests in one place
- Review and approve/reject leave requests
- Manage shift swap requests
- Approve time adjustments and corrections
- Handle overtime requests
- Process benefit selections and changes
- Track approval history
- Generate approval reports
- Receive notifications for new requests
- Set delegation for when you're away

As a manager, approvals are critical to payroll accuracy, scheduling, and compliance. Your timely approvals ensure employee satisfaction and accurate records.

## Your Manager Approval Responsibilities

### Approval Authority

As a manager, you can approve:
- ✅ Leave requests (Vacation, Sick, Personal)
- ✅ Shift swap requests
- ✅ Time adjustment requests
- ✅ Overtime requests (if within budget)
- ✅ Work-from-home requests
- ✅ Flexible schedule requests

### Cannot Approve (Escalate to HR)

- ❌ Promotion or compensation changes
- ❌ Policy exceptions or violations
- ❌ Disciplinary actions
- ❌ Termination or leave of absence
- ❌ Benefits enrollment (limited authority)

### SLA (Service Level Agreement)

**Approval Timeframe**:
- **Standard Requests**: 24-48 hours
- **Urgent Requests**: Same day if possible
- **Emergency Requests**: Within 4 hours

Delayed approvals impact employee satisfaction, payroll accuracy, and scheduling.

You can review and approve requests from the Kando mobile app, not just web.

**Accessing your approvals**:
1. Open the Kando app and go to the **Requests** tab
2. Tap **My Approvals** (only visible if you have approval authority)
3. Tap any request to review its details, then **Approve** or **Reject**

**What you'll see**: Requester name, request type, requested date, and date submitted. Open a request to see an **Approved By** section showing who's signed off so far, for requests needing multiple approvals.

## Understanding Overtime

### What is Overtime?

**Overtime (OT)** = Work hours BEYOND the standard working hours (usually 40 hours/week)

**Key Concept**: Employees are paid at a premium rate for overtime instead of regular rate

### How Overtime Works

**Basic Structure**:
```
Standard Work Week: 40 hours
├─ Monday-Thursday: 8 hrs/day = 32 hours
├─ Friday: 8 hrs/day = 8 hours
└─ Total: 40 hours (NO overtime)

Extended Work Week: 50 hours
├─ Monday-Thursday: 8 hrs/day = 32 hours
├─ Friday: 10 hrs/day = 10 hours
├─ Saturday: 8 hrs/day = 8 hours
└─ Total: 50 hours (10 hours = OVERTIME)
```

**OT Calculation**:
```
Regular Pay: 40 hrs × PHP 125/hr = PHP 5,000
Overtime Pay: 10 hrs × PHP 187.50/hr (1.5x) = PHP 1,875
Total Pay: PHP 6,875 (instead of PHP 5,000)
Difference: +PHP 1,875 extra pay
```

### Overtime Pay Rates

Different rates apply depending on context:

| Overtime Type | Rate | Example |
|---------------|------|---------|
| **Regular OT** (weekday beyond 40) | 1.25x or 1.5x | 10 hrs × PHP 187.50 = PHP 1,875 |
| **Night OT** (11 PM - 6 AM) | 1.5x or 2x | 8 hrs × PHP 250 = PHP 2,000 |
| **Holiday OT** (worked on holiday) | 2x or 2.5x | 8 hrs × PHP 250 = PHP 2,000 |
| **Rest Day OT** (worked on rest day) | 1.5x or 2x | 8 hrs × PHP 250 = PHP 2,000 |

**Your Policy**: Ask HR for your organization's specific OT multipliers.

### Types of Overtime Requests

#### 1. Requested/Planned Overtime

**What It Is**: Employee asks permission to work extra hours

**Process**:
1. Employee submits OT request (with reason)
2. You review and approve/reject
3. If approved: Employee works the hours
4. Hours appear on timesheet
5. Paid at OT rate on payslip

**Typical Reasons**:
- Project deadline approaching
- Critical work needed urgently
- Backlog to clear
- Special event or peak season

**Example**:
```
Request: Feb 20 (Friday) - work until 8 PM (2 extra hours)
Reason: Quarterly report due Monday
Approval: YES (deadline critical, no budget issues)
Result: 2 hours OT on Feb 20 payslip
```

#### 2. Unplanned Overtime

**What It Is**: Employee works extra hours without prior approval (emergencies, urgent needs)

**Process**:
1. Employee works the hours and clocks in
2. Timesheet shows extra hours automatically
3. You review during timesheet approval
4. Can approve or request changes
5. Paid at OT rate if approved

**Example**:
```
Situation: System outage Friday evening, must fix immediately
Employee: Works Friday 5 PM - 11 PM (6 unplanned OT hours)
Process: Clocks the hours, timesheet shows extra
Your Review: "Critical system issue, emergency work justified"
Result: APPROVE - 6 hours OT paid
```

### Manager's Role in Overtime Approval

**What You Decide**:
1. ✅ Business need justified?
2. ✅ Budget available for OT costs?
3. ✅ Employee capable/safe working OT?
4. ✅ Is this one-time or recurring pattern?
5. ✅ Impact on rest and recovery?

**Important Considerations**:

**Budget**: Each team has OT budget
```
Monthly Budget: PHP 5,000
Used: PHP 2,500 (so far)
Remaining: PHP 2,500 (can approve)

Request: 10 hours OT = PHP 1,875
Decision: YES (fits in remaining budget)
```

**Frequency**: Watch for patterns
```
Employee working OT every week = Sign of:
├─ Workload too high?
├─ Staffing shortage?
├─ Employee inefficiency?
└─ Burnout risk?

Action: Address root cause, not just approve OT
```

**Employee Health**: Don't overwork people
```
Worked OT: 20 hours this month
+ 30 hours regular = 50 hours total
Already above 40, now 50 = Burnout risk
Decision: REJECT and redistribute work
```

### Common Overtime Scenarios

#### Scenario 1: Project Deadline Approaching

**Situation**: Project due in 2 days, team needs to work weekend

**Analysis**:
- Deadline: Real and business-critical ✅
- Budget: PHP 8,000 available, needs PHP 3,000 ✅
- Timeline: 2 people × 8 hrs × PHP 187.50 = PHP 3,000
- Recovery: Can give Monday off to compensate ✅

**Decision**: APPROVE
```
Message: "Approved for Sat-Sun. Please take Monday off to recover."
Pay: Double pay for weekend work + day off in lieu
```

#### Scenario 2: Employee Pattern (Chronic OT)

**Situation**: Same employee working 10+ hours OT/week for 3 weeks

**Analysis**:
- Pattern: Not emergency, recurring ⚠️
- Cause: Understaffed team or slow worker?
- Impact: Burnout risk, fatigue, errors ⚠️
- Solution: Add staff, redistribute, training ⚠️

**Decision**: APPROVE CURRENT but address issue
```
Message: "Approved this week, but let's discuss this pattern.
I'll escalate to hire temp or redistribute work."
Action: Schedule meeting with employee and HR
```

#### Scenario 3: Budget Exhausted

**Situation**: OT request but monthly budget already spent

**Analysis**:
- Budget: PHP 5,000 limit
- Used: PHP 5,000 (100%)
- Remaining: PHP 0 ❌
- Request: 10 hours = PHP 1,875 (exceeds budget)

**Decision**: REJECT and escalate
```
Message: "OT budget exhausted this month. Submitting to director
for budget increase authorization. You'll hear by EOD."
Action: Escalate to director for exception
```

#### Scenario 4: Unplanned Emergency OT

**Situation**: Production system down Friday evening, employee works 6 unplanned OT hours

**Analysis**:
- Emergency: Yes ✅
- Justified: System down, critical work ✅
- Budget: Still have room ✅
- Recovery: System restored by 11 PM ✅

**Decision**: APPROVE
```
Message: "Great work handling the emergency. 6 hours OT approved
and will be reflected on next payslip."
Documentation: Note in system for audit trail
```

### Approving Overtime Requests

**Step-by-Step**:

1. **Review Request Details**
   - Date(s) of OT
   - Number of hours
   - Reason provided
   - Employee's current OT this month

2. **Check Budget**
   ```
   Remaining Budget: PHP 5,000
   Request Cost: 10 hrs × PHP 187.50 = PHP 1,875
   After Approval: PHP 3,125 remaining
   Decision: Budget OK ✅
   ```

3. **Assess Business Need**
   - Critical deadline?
   - Emergency?
   - Reasonable?
   - Or unnecessary?

4. **Check Employee Status**
   - Already worked a lot this week?
   - Health/fatigue concern?
   - Capable of extra work?

5. **Make Decision**
   - APPROVE: "Critical deadline, budget available, employee willing"
   - REJECT: "Budget exhausted, can reschedule for next period"
   - MODIFY: "Can approve 4 hours instead of 8"
   - ESCALATE: "Request exceeds my authority"

6. **Communicate**
   - Message employee clearly
   - Explain decision
   - Set expectations
   - Note any conditions

### Overtime Policies and Limits

**Common Policy Limits**:

| Policy Aspect | Typical Limit | Reason |
|---------------|--------------|--------|
| **Weekly OT** | Max 10-15 hours | Prevent burnout |
| **Monthly OT** | Max 40-60 hours | Budget control |
| **Per-Request** | Max 8 hours | Encourage breaks |
| **Consecutive Days** | Max 5 days | Rest requirement |
| **Without Approval** | Max 2 hours/week | Prevent abuse |

**Your Responsibility**: Enforce your organization's policy consistently

**Ask HR**:
- What's the OT policy?
- What are the limits?
- How do I escalate when exceeded?
- What's the approval process?

### Impact on Payroll

**How OT Appears on Payslip**:

```
EARNINGS:
├─ Basic Pay (40 hrs): PHP 5,000
├─ Overtime (10 hrs): PHP 1,875 ← At premium rate
└─ Gross: PHP 6,875

TAXES (Higher because more earnings):
├─ Income Tax: Higher due to extra pay
└─ SSS/PhilHealth: Higher (based on gross)

NET PAY: Higher than normal
```

**Important**: OT pushes employee into higher tax bracket

**Example Tax Impact**:
```
Without OT: PHP 5,000 gross → PHP 4,200 net
With OT: PHP 6,875 gross → PHP 5,600 net (not +PHP 1,875)
Why? Extra PHP 1,875 taxed at higher rate
```

### Overtime Approval Decision Tree

```
Is OT Request Received?
    ↓
Budget Available?
├─ NO → REJECT (budget exhausted)
└─ YES ↓
   Business Need Justified?
   ├─ NO → REJECT (unnecessary)
   └─ YES ↓
      Employee Status OK?
      ├─ NO (fatigued) → REJECT (health concern)
      └─ YES ↓
         WITHIN YOUR AUTHORITY?
         ├─ NO → ESCALATE
         └─ YES ↓

```

### Tips for Managing Overtime

✅ **DO**:
- **Approve emergency OT immediately** (don't delay)
- **Track budget closely** (know remaining allocation)
- **Watch for patterns** (don't let OT become norm)
- **Communicate clearly** (why approved or rejected)
- **Document decisions** (audit trail for compliance)
- **Encourage rest/recovery** (days off after heavy OT)
- **Address root causes** (if OT recurring, fix underlying issue)

❌ **DON'T**:
- Don't use OT as staffing substitute (hire instead)
- Don't exceed budget without escalation
- Don't approve without business justification
- Don't ignore burnout risks (employee fatigue)
- Don't show favoritism (approve for some, reject for others)
- Don't allow excessive OT without recovery days
- Don't approve OT during peak leave season (doubles burden)

## Step-by-Step Approval Workflows

### Workflow 1: Approve Leave Request

**Step 1: Receive Notification**
- Email or in-app notification: "Maria requested leave for Feb 17-18"
- Click notification link to review

**Step 2: Review Request**
```
Information to Check:
☐ Employee name and ID correct?
☐ Leave type appropriate?
☐ Dates and duration reasonable?
☐ Employee has sufficient balance?
☐ Team coverage available?
☐ Business impact acceptable?
☐ Reason provided?
☐ Advance notice given?
☐ Medical certificate attached (if required)?
```

**Step 3: Make Decision**

**IF APPROVING**:
1. Click **Approve** button
2. Add optional manager note:
   - "Approved. Please hand over Project X tasks."
   - "Enjoy your vacation!"
3. Click **Confirm**

**IF REJECTING**:
1. Click **Reject** button
2. Provide clear reason:
   - "Insufficient team coverage that week"
   - "Timeline conflicts with quarterly review"
   - "Requested dates less than 2 weeks (see policy)"
3. Suggest alternative:
   - "Can you request Feb 24-25 instead?"
   - "Would 1 day on Feb 17 work?"
4. Click **Submit**

**Step 4: Outcome**
- ✅ APPROVED: Employee notified, leaves added to schedule
- ❌ REJECTED: Employee notified, can revise request

**Timeline**: Complete within 24-48 hours

### Workflow 2: Approve Shift Swap

**Step 1: Receive Notification**
- "Maria requested to swap shifts with Jessica"
- Both parties must have already agreed

**Step 2: Review Request**
```
Verification Checklist:
☐ Both employees agreed (both checked yes)?
☐ Same shift type (both morning shifts)?
☐ Coverage maintained (still adequate staff)?
☐ No scheduling conflicts?
☐ Reason seems legitimate (if provided)?
☐ No abuse pattern (frequent swaps)?
```

**Step 3: Make Decision**

**Example Coverage Check**:
```
BEFORE SWAP:
- Thursday Morning: Maria, John, Robert (3 people) ✅
- Thursday Afternoon: Jessica, Ana (2 people) ✅

AFTER SWAP (if approved):
- Thursday Morning: Jessica, John, Robert (3 people) ✅
- Thursday Afternoon: Maria, Ana (2 people) ✅

Result: SAFE TO APPROVE ✅
```

**Step 4: Approve**
1. Click **Approve**
2. Add note (optional): "Approved"
3. Click **Confirm**
4. Both employees notified
5. Schedule automatically updated

**Timeline**: Same day ideally

### Workflow 3: Approve Time Adjustment

**Step 1: Review Adjustment**
```
Information Needed:
☐ Date of original time entry
☐ Original recorded time
☐ Adjustment amount (add/subtract hours)
☐ Reason provided
☐ Supporting evidence
☐ Payment impact
```

**Step 2: Investigate**
- Ask yourself: "Is this reasonable?"
- Check for patterns: Has employee made similar requests?
- Verify reason: Does explanation make sense?
- Review evidence: Medical note, supervisor observation, system issue?

**Step 3: Decision Logic**
```
Is there strong evidence? → YES → Approve
                         → NO → Request more info or Reject

Is this first occurrence? → YES → Approve (benefit of doubt)
                        → NO → Investigate pattern

Is payment impact significant? → YES → Escalate to HR/Finance
                             → NO → Approve if reasonable
```

**Step 4: Approve or Reject**
- **Approve**: Add note "Evidence verified" or "Confirmed with employee"
- **Reject**: Provide clear reason "Insufficient evidence" or "Policy violation"

## Best Practices

✅ **DO**:
- **Review promptly** (within 24 hours of submission)
- **Request clarification** if information is unclear
- **Check team coverage** before approving time changes
- **Provide clear reasoning** when rejecting
- **Maintain consistency** (apply rules fairly to all employees)
- **Document decisions** in system notes
- **Follow policies** strictly (no exceptions without escalation)
- **Communicate clearly** with employees about decisions
- **Track approval history** for audits and disputes
- **Set notifications** so you don't miss requests
- **Delegate when away** (assign to skip-level manager)

❌ **DON'T**:
- Approve requests without proper review
- Show favoritism (approve some, reject similar for others)
- Make exceptions without policy justification
- Forget to provide feedback/reasoning
- Delay approvals (impacts employee satisfaction and payroll)
- Approve overlapping leave without team discussion
- Ignore patterns (repeated requests, potential policy abuse)
- Approve without considering team impact
- Share request details beyond need-to-know
- Lose track of approval deadlines

## Common Approval Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Sick Leave (No Documentation)

**Situation**: Employee calls in sick, submits leave request in afternoon

**Analysis**:
- Policy: Sick leave doesn't require advance notice
- Documentation: 1-day sick leave typically doesn't need certificate
- Team impact: Already covered for today
- Frequency: First occurrence this month

**Decision**: APPROVE
- Clear justification: Policy allows immediate sick leave
- Add note: "Approved for today"

### Scenario 2: Last-Minute Vacation Request

**Situation**: Employee requests 1-week vacation starting Monday (submitted Friday)

**Analysis**:
- Advance Notice: Only 2 days (policy usually requires 2 weeks)
- Team Impact: Last-minute coverage difficult
- Balance: Employee has 8 days available
- Reason: "Family emergency visit"

**Decision**: DISCUSS FIRST
1. Message employee: "Normally we need 2 weeks notice. What's the situation?"
2. If legitimate emergency: Can make exception
3. If just poor planning: Suggest different dates
4. Document decision and rationale

### Scenario 3: Multiple Team Members Request Same Dates

**Situation**: 3 out of 7 team members request Feb 20 off

**Analysis**:
- Team Size: 7 people
- Requests: 3 people (43% of team)
- Minimum Coverage: Probably need 4-5 people
- Coverage Impact: INSUFFICIENT

**Decisions**:
1. **First Request** (received earliest): APPROVE
2. **Second Request**: APPROVE (still have minimum coverage)
3. **Third Request**: REJECT "Insufficient team coverage. Can you choose different date?"
4. Explain to employee: "With 3 already approved, we don't have coverage."

### Scenario 4: Frequent Adjuster

**Situation**: Same employee requesting 3rd time adjustment this month

**Analysis**:
- Frequency: Pattern emerging (3 in 1 month)
- Pattern Suggests: Clock-in/out issues or dishonesty
- First 2 Adjustments: Were approved
- Policy Violation: If employee deliberately not clocking in

**Actions**:
1. Reject request: "Your 3rd adjustment this month. Let's discuss."
2. Meet with employee: "I've approved 2 adjustments already. What's happening?"
3. Investigation: Technical issue? Policy violation? Misunderstanding?
4. Resolution: Training on clock-in process, or address policy issue
5. Document: Log in system for future reference

### Scenario 5: Manager on Vacation

**Situation**: You're on leave but requests continue arriving

**Before You Leave**:
1. Navigate to **Settings** → **Approvals**
2. Click **Delegate Authority**
3. Select **Temporary Manager**: Skip-level manager or peer
4. Set dates: While you're on leave
5. Types to delegate: All types (leave, swaps, adjustments)
6. Manager automatically handles approvals
7. Notify team: "John will review requests while I'm away"

### Requesting Information from Employees

**When to Request More Info**:
- Missing documentation (medical cert)
- Unclear reason for request
- Conflicting information
- Additional details needed

**Process**:
1. Click **Request Information** button
2. Write message to employee:
   ```
   "Please provide medical certificate for sick leave request.
    Standard for leaves over 2 days. Once received, I can approve."
   ```
3. Employee receives message and can respond
4. Request status changes to "Awaiting Employee Response"
5. Doesn't count against your response SLA (paused)

## Multi-Step Approval Workflows

### What Are Multi-Step Approvals?

**Multi-step approvals** mean that a single request requires approval from **multiple people** before it's fully approved. This is used for requests that need:
- Multiple levels of authority
- Cross-functional approval
- High-value or high-impact decisions
- Compliance and audit requirements

**Key Concept**: A request may need YOUR approval AND a higher manager's approval AND HR's approval before it's implemented.

### When Are Multi-Step Approvals Used?

**Common Scenarios**:

| Request Type | Why Multiple Approvers | Example |
|--------------|------------------------|---------|
| **High Overtime** | Budget control | >20 hours requires director approval |
| **Long Leave** | Coverage planning | >2 weeks requires skip-level manager |
| **Salary Advance** | Finance approval | Requires HR + Finance sign-off |
| **Exceptional Time Off** | Policy exception | Requires Manager + HR Admin + Director |
| **Compensation Change** | High-impact decision | Requires Manager + HR + Finance |
| **Shift Pattern Change** | Scheduling impact | Requires Manager + Scheduling + HR |

**Example**: A 3-week vacation request might need:
1. ✅ **Your approval** (direct manager) - covers team
2. ✅ **Skip-level manager approval** (your manager) - coverage planning
3. ✅ **HR approval** - policy compliance

### Sequential vs. Parallel Approvals

#### Sequential Approval (Step-by-Step)

**Sequential** = One person approves, then next person, then next person (in order)

```
Request Submitted
        ↓
Step 1: You Review & Approve
        ↓
Step 2: Your Manager Reviews & Approves
        ↓
Step 3: HR Reviews & Approves
        ↓
APPROVED ✅
```

**Timeline**: Takes longer (3 review periods)

**Example**:
- Mon: You approve
- Tue: Your manager approves
- Wed: HR approves
- Total: 3 days to complete

**Use Case**: Financial or policy-sensitive requests where each layer has distinct authority

#### Parallel Approval (Simultaneous)

**Parallel** = All approvers review at the same time (not waiting for each other)

```
Request Submitted
        ↓
Step 1: You Review & Approve (simultaneously with...)
Step 2: HR Approves (simultaneously with...)
Step 3: Scheduling Approves
        ↓
APPROVED ✅ (when all approve)
```

**Timeline**: Faster (single review period)

**Example**:
- Mon: All 3 approvers notified
- Tue: All 3 can approve independently
- Total: 1 day to complete

**Use Case**: When approvers don't depend on each other's decision

### AND vs. OR Logic

#### AND Logic (All Must Approve)

**AND** = ALL approvers must say "YES" for approval

```
Request approved IF:
  ✅ Manager approves AND
  ✅ HR approves AND
  ✅ Finance approves
```

**Result**: Even ONE "NO" blocks the entire request

**Example: High Overtime Request**
- Manager says: ✅ YES (covers team)
- Finance says: ❌ NO (over budget)
- **Result**: REJECTED (even though manager approved)

**Common For**: Financial requests, policy exceptions, high-impact changes

#### OR Logic (Any Can Approve)

**OR** = At least ONE approver must say "YES" for approval

```
Request approved IF:
  Manager approves OR
  Skip-level manager approves OR
  HR Admin approves
```

**Result**: ONE "YES" is enough (unless all reject)

**Example: Shift Swap When Manager Away**
- Your Manager: No response (on leave)
- Skip-level Manager: ✅ YES
- **Result**: APPROVED (skip-level is enough)

**Common For**: Coverage situations, emergency approvals, delegation scenarios

### Your Role in Multi-Step Approval

As a direct manager, you're typically **Step 1** of multi-step approvals.

**Your Responsibilities**:

1. **Review your step promptly**: Don't delay other approvers
2. **Provide clear decision**: Approve or reject with notes
3. **Understand your scope**: What authority do you have?
4. **Don't assume downstream approval**: Your YES doesn't guarantee final YES
5. **Communicate to employee**: Explain if you approve but higher levels may reject

**Example Conversation**:
```
Employee: "Did you approve my leave?"
You: "Yes, I approved. It's now with my manager for final approval."
Employee: "When will I know?"
You: "Our director reviews these on Fridays, so by end of week."
```

### Multi-Step Approval Scenarios

#### Scenario 1: You Approve, Higher Manager Rejects

**Situation**: You approved leave for Maria (3 weeks), but your manager rejected it.

**What Happened**:
1. Maria requested 3-week leave
2. ✅ You approved (team coverage OK)
3. ❌ Your manager rejected (company policy - max 2 weeks without director approval)
4. Maria is REJECTED overall

**Your Next Action**:
1. Notify Maria: "I approved, but higher management requires 2 weeks max"
2. Suggest solution: "Can you split into 2-week + 1-week later?"
3. Or escalate: "Request director exception with your reason"
4. Lesson: Different approval levels have different rules

#### Scenario 2: OR Logic with Delegation

**Situation**: You're on leave. Request comes in.

**Sequential Process**:
1. Request submitted for shift swap
2. System tries to notify YOU (but you're away)
3. Your approval is skipped or delegated
4. Skip-level manager or HR approves instead
5. Request doesn't wait for your return

**You**: Don't need to approve (delegation handles it)

#### Scenario 3: AND Logic with Finance

**Situation**: John requests high overtime (25 hours).

**Requirements**:
- ✅ Your approval needed (team coverage)
- ✅ Finance approval needed (overtime budget)

**Scenario A - You Say YES, Finance Says NO**:
- **Result**: REJECTED (finance budget constraint)
- John notified: "Approved by manager but finance doesn't have budget"

**Scenario B - You Say NO, Finance Says YES**:
- **Result**: REJECTED (manager coverage concern)
- John notified: "Manager indicated coverage issue"

**Scenario C - Both Say YES**:
- **Result**: APPROVED ✅
- John can work overtime

### Escalation in Multi-Step Workflows

**Escalation** = Moving request up the approval chain

**When to Escalate**:
- You can't approve (outside your authority)
- You approve but want higher-level review
- Request is urgent and needs faster approval
- Request is complex and needs expert decision

**How to Escalate**:
1. Review request in your approval queue
2. Look for **Escalate** or **Send to Manager** button
3. Add note: "This requires higher authority" or "Unusual situation"
4. Submit - request moves to next level

**Example**:
- Policy exception (you can't authorize, HR can)
- Unprecedented request (you need director guidance)
- Conflict between two departments

### Tips for Managing Multi-Step Approvals

✅ **DO**:
- **Act promptly**: Don't delay by holding request at your step
- **Provide clear notes**: Explain your decision to help downstream approvers
- **Escalate when needed**: Don't approve requests outside your authority
- **Track timing**: Know how long each step takes
- **Document pattern**: If same type keeps being rejected, notify employee
- **Communicate to employee**: Explain approval is multi-step and timeline
- **Check your scope**: Understand what you CAN and CAN'T approve

❌ **DON'T**:
- Don't approve requests you don't understand (ask first)
- Don't assume your approval is final
- Don't ignore requests while on leave (delegate first)
- Don't provide generic notes ("looks good") for complex requests
- Don't give mixed signals ("I approve but I'm not sure")
- Don't make exceptions without higher authority

### Understanding Approval Permissions

**Approval Types You Have Authority For**:
- Leave requests (standard policy)
- Shift swaps (team coverage)
- Time adjustments (minor corrections)
- Overtime (within budget)

**NOT Your Authority** (escalate to HR):
- Policy exceptions
- Compensation changes
- Disciplinary actions
- Contract modifications
- Benefits changes

**When in Doubt**: Escalate to HR. Better to escalate than to approve something you don't have authority for.

## Troubleshooting

**Problem**: "I can't find a request I remember seeing"
**Cause**: Request already processed (approved/rejected) and moved to history
**Solution**:
1. Go to **Approvals** → **History**
2. Search by employee name or date
3. Verify approval status
4. View details and notes

**Problem**: "Employee says I rejected their request but I don't remember"
**Cause**: Unclear communication or employee confusion
**Solution**:
1. Check **History** for that employee
2. View exact rejection reason you provided
3. Share decision with employee
4. If legitimate error, re-review and approve if appropriate
5. Document follow-up

**Problem**: "Can't approve request - button not working"
**Cause**: Permission issue, request already processed, or system error
**Solution**:
1. Verify you're the assigned manager
2. Refresh page to reload
3. Check if request was already approved/rejected
4. Try different browser
5. Contact support if issue persists

**Problem**: "Approved request but it didn't implement in schedule/payroll"
**Cause**: System processing delay or integration issue
**Solution**:
1. Wait 24 hours (system may need to process)
2. Refresh page to check status
3. Verify approval was actually confirmed (check history)
4. Contact support if not updated after 24 hours

**Problem**: "Employee says they submitted request but I don't see it"
**Cause**: Technical issue, wrong manager assigned, or notification delay
**Solution**:
1. Ask employee for submission date/time
2. Check your request inbox for that time period
3. Verify you're correct manager for this employee
4. Check employee's copy (do they see it submitted?)
5. Employee may need to resubmit if system error

## Advanced Approval Scenarios

### Conditional Approvals

**Conditional Approval** = Approval with specific conditions employee must meet

**Example**: Leave request with handover requirement
```
APPROVE with conditions:
├─ Must submit project handover by Friday
├─ Must ensure all client calls completed
└─ OK to proceed only if conditions met
```

**How It Works**:
1. Click **APPROVE**
2. Add conditions in notes: "Handover documentation required"
3. Message employee with conditions
4. Employee acknowledges and completes conditions
5. You confirm conditions met
6. Leave processes

### Exception Approvals

**Exception** = Deviation from standard policy

**Scenario**: Employee requests 5-week vacation (policy = 3 weeks max)

**Process**:
1. System detects policy violation
2. You can't approve (exceeds authority)
3. Click **ESCALATE** → Director
4. Add note: "5-week vacation - policy exception requested"
5. Director decides: Override or enforce?
6. You notified of final decision

**Authority Levels**:
- **You can approve**: Standard requests within policy
- **Escalate to Director**: Policy exceptions, high amounts
- **HR only**: Compensation changes, benefits, disciplinary

### Approval Patterns

**Monitor for Patterns**:
```
Red Flag 1: Same employee constant requests
└─ Action: Talk to them about workload/planning

Red Flag 2: Clustering around holidays
└─ Action: Plan staffing in advance

Red Flag 3: High rejection rate from peer
└─ Action: Ensure consistency across team

Red Flag 4: Urgent requests pattern
└─ Action: Coach on planning ahead
```

**Using Analytics**:
1. Go to **Reports** → **Approval Analytics**
2. View by employee, type, time, manager
3. Identify outliers
4. Take action if needed

### Approval Delegation

**When You're Away**:

1. Go to **Settings** → **Delegate Approvals**
2. Select manager (skip-level or peer)
3. Date range: Your leave dates
4. Request types: All or specific
5. Click **Save**
6. Delegate manager receives your requests

**Temporary Reassignment**:
- Requests still sent to you
- You click **Reassign**
- Select substitute manager
- Original employee notified

### Handling Urgent Approvals

**Urgent Request** = Fast approval needed (medical, emergency)

**Standard SLA**: 24-48 hours
**Urgent SLA**: 2-4 hours

**Process**:
1. Request marked "URGENT"
2. Goes to top of queue
3. Process immediately (same day)
4. Call employee if critical
5. Notify immediately

**Example**:
```
9:00 AM: Urgent leave submitted (medical appointment today)
9:30 AM: You approve
10:00 AM: Employee called with approval
10:30 AM: Employee leaves for appointment
```

### Approval Conflicts

**Conflict** = Competing priorities

**Scenario**: Shift swap request
- ✅ Coverage: Adequate (enough people)
- ❌ Skill: Other person lacks required skill

**Decision Process**:
1. Weigh importance (quality vs. coverage)
2. Assess impact (customer, operations, team)
3. Seek alternative (pair with experienced, provide training)
4. Document decision
5. Communicate clearly

**Decision**: "REJECT: Customer requires senior service" OR "APPROVE: Provide training support"

### Best Practices for Advanced Approvals

✅ **DO**:
- Document complex decisions (explain reasoning)
- Escalate early (don't approve outside authority)
- Set clear conditions (be specific)
- Handle urgency promptly (don't delay)
- Monitor patterns (watch for issues)
- Maintain consistency (treat fairly)
- Track exceptions (for audit trail)

❌ **DON'T**:
- Approve outside authority (escalate instead)
- Ignore patterns (address early)
- Set vague conditions (be specific)
- Delay urgent requests (fast-track)
- Favor some employees (treat equally)
- Change rules mid-month (apply consistently)
- Forget to document (for compliance)
